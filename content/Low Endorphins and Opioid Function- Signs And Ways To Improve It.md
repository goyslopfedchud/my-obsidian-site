## Endorphins, Enkephalins & Opioids

| Opioid<br>receptor/subtypes | Endogenous<br>ligands                                     | Effects of receptor-<br>ligand interactions                                         |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mu (μ)                      | β-endorphins<br><br>enkephalins                           | euphoria, <br>constipation, miosis<br><br>analgesia, <br>respiratory <br>depression |
| Delta (δ)                   | enkephalins<br>(met/leu-<br>enkephalins),<br>β-endorphins | euphoria, analgesia                                                                 |
| Kappa (κ)                   | dynorphins<br><br>enkephalins                             | dysphoria<br><br>analgesia                                                          |
	- Endorphins and enkephalins are considered to be indigenous neurotransmitters, meaning they're synthesized in your body activating the opioid receptors
	- We have μ-opioid, δ-opioid, and κ-opioids receptors
	- Endorphins is generally longer acting and it has more broad action, whereas enkephalins is more targeted and is shorter acting
	- Opioids agonists like morphine and opioid antagonist like naloxone, which is used to reverse opioids induced respiratory depression
## Signs and Symptoms of Opioid Disruption
	1. Physical fatigue
	2. Low muscle endurance
	3. Low pain threshold
	4. Low pain tolerance
	5. Muscle ache, joint pain
	6. ADD, lack of motivation
	7. Anhedonia & reduced creativity
	8. Eat or crave a lot of dairy and bread
	9. General malaise and PEM
	10. Prone to opioid addiction
	11. Trouble falling asleep
	12. Cold intolerance
	13. Poor immunity
	14. Diarrhea
	- Physical fatigue and low endurance are probably going to be the most prominent symptoms: low pain threshold, low pain tolerance, especially if there's achiness joint pains
	- ADd and anhedonia is mostly thought to be dopamine related, but it may just be as equally related to low endorphins 
	- The amphetamine based ADD stimulants also release endorphins whereas other dopamine agonists like rosageline, amantadine don't always get the jobs done in this field
	- General malaise and postexertional malaise, you generally don't feel good in your body, nauseous, tired, maybe achy, and have a form of exercise intolerance
	- When someone with a functional opioid receptor takes opioid painkillers, they'll likely feel drowsy, nauseous, maybe even dysphoric
	- Someone with a dysfunctional opioid system, you have a higher chance of having opioid use disorder
	- If you have more than 8 symptoms listed here, that's a pretty indicator that you may be struggling with systemic low endorphins
	- The symptoms that you can just pull up from google are overly generalized, and they may be inaccurate
## Foods that can Disrupt Opioid Function
	1. Casein (casomorphin)
	2. Gluten (exorphin)
	3. Soybean (soymorphin)
	4. Nightshade vegetables (tomato, bell pepper, potatoe, etc)
	5. Refined sugar & high glycemic foods
	6. Alcohol
	7. MSG
	- See if you might be self medicating yourself with these foods
	- Some of them can mimic endorphins, morphine and temporarily make you feel good but it'll have the swinging pendulum effect and make you feel worse afterwards
	- If you just avoid these foods, then you're probably not going to need any supplements or medications to increase endorphins

	12. Selank
	13. Semax
	- If you insist on doing supplements or medications then talk to your doctor first

	- Butyrate can increase the number of μ-opioids receptors

	- Cayenne pepper only lasts like an hour or two
	- Low dose naltrexone is a good one to ask your doctor about, it is getting easier to get prescribed for; start at very small dose like a half milligram or one milligram, and make sure to take it consistently, everyday or every second day for it to take effect
	- Micro dosing buprenorphine is quite effective and you can just take it periodically when you want to and the only downside to this is that you can easily overdose on it-  People with opioid addiction takes around 20 - 25 milligrams, but with microdosing you're taking around 25 micrograms, which is very hard to do; if you do too much, you just get sometimes overstimulated, but most likely nauseous
	- Kratom, has positive and negative effects, like irritability or anger- Definitely try to explore other routs first
## Negative Effects of Opioids
	1. Nausea
	2. Respiratory depression
	3. Constipation
	4. Irritability & rage
	5. Mania
	6. Hallucination & psychosis
	7. Low sex drive
	8. Itch, increased histamine
	9. Difficulty reaching orgasm
	10. Insomnia
	- Nausea most certainly means you're taken too much
	- If your breathing slows down a lot, then you've most definitely taken way too much
	- Irritability and rage is quite common, constipation as well
	- Activating opioid receptors can have some mild anti psychosis effects but it can cause mania and hallucination as well 
	- Low sex drive is common too
