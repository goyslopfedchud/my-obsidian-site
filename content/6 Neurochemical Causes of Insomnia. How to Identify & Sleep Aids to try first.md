
## Excess Catecholamines
	1. Difficulty falling asleep, wide awake in bed, light sleep
	2. Generalized anxiety
	3. Obsessive rumination 
	4. Elevated BP & HR
	5. Chest tension, palpitation
	6. Hypervigilance, jumpy
	7. Paranoia
	8. Mania
	9. Anger, rage
	10. PTSD
	- Catecholamines are dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine: you'll likely going to have difficulty falling asleep, might have generalized anxiety, elevated blood pressure and heart rate, possibly chest tension and palpitations
	- Anger and rage is also a good marker
## High Histamine
	1. Itchy skin
	2. Allergies
	3. Irritable, neurotic
	4. Hot flashes
	5. Puffy eyes
	6. Runny nose
	7. Brain fog
	- Your body has a system which releases epinephrine when it becomes high in histamine
	- Think of aphlaxis reaction from histamine overload like peanut allergies; that will cause your body release epinephrine and also you can take epinephrin shots to clear out the histamine 
	- The most notable symptoms will likely be itchiness and brain fog, possibly runny nose 
## 5HT2A Overactivation
	1. Trouble falling asleep, but not always stressful
	2. Situational and symbolic anxiety, spiritual overwhelm
	3. Poor impulse control
	4. Racing mind, and illogical thinking
	5. Chronic fatigue
	6. Dissociation, derealization
	7. Suicidal, homicidal
	- Serotonin 2A receptor, receptor for neurotransmitter serotonin; you're likely gonna have trouble falling asleep, situational and symbolix anxiety, impulsivity
## Low GABA / High Glutamate
	1. Easily aroused during sleep, disturbing dreams
	2. Generalized and anticipatory anxiety
	3. Restless, asathisia
	4. Brain on fire
	5. Tired but wired
	6. Reduced emotional intelligence
	7. Tremors
	- GABA issues are one of the most common causes of insomnia; difficulty falling asleep and staying asleep, possibly have bad dreams
	- You might frequently get jolted awake with like sudden muscle contractions
	- Generalized anxiety or anxiety from anticipating something in the future event, although when it comes to it you may perform rather well
## HPA Axis Dysregulation
	1. Tired during the day, awake at night
	2. Waking up between 22 - 01 after falling asleep
	3. Poor temperature regulation
	4. Abdominal fat accumulation
	5. Autoimmunity
	6. PTSD
	7. Easily overwhelmed by stress
	8. Frequent colds & flus
	- It's hypothalamus pituitary and adrenal axis dysregulation
	- You'll likely notice being tired during the day, but feel awake at night
	- Heat tolerance or say during the winter, at a room temperature, where everyone is wearing T-shirts but you're wearing sweater
## Low endorphins
	1. Achy muscle or joints
	2. Physical fatigue
	3. Quick to muscle fatigue during exercise
	4. Low pain tolerance & threshold
	5. Lack of creativity or anhedonia 
	6. Lot motivation, ADD / ADHD
	7. Restless legs (RLS)
	- Achy muscles and joints, physical and muscle fatigue, not mental one
## Lists of Medications and other Sleep Aids (brackets: what neurotransmitters they are know to affect)
## Quetiapine, Olanzapine (5HT2A, dopamine)
	- Procs: potent
	- Cons: fatigue, depression, and anger the day after, hunger & weight gain
	- Quetiapine, often known as seroquel and olanzapine are antipsychotic drugs, potent, but they can make you really tired and prone to anger
## Prazosin (adrenaline)
	- Pros: general anti anxiety effects
	- Cons: need to be taken continuously
	- An alpha adrenergic receptor antagonist, definitely not something you want to try as your first
	- It would be good if you have generalized anxiety maybe like heart issues but it has to be taken continuously 
	- Alpha blocker and other adrenaline blockers have some nasty side effects
## L-Methionine or SAMe (catecholamines)
	- Pros: easy to obtain, safe to mix
	- Cons: works only if you have low COMT
	- S-adenosylmethionine: supplements, easy to obtain
	- Probably gonna work only if you have low COMP, catecholamines o methyltransferase- An enzyme that breaks down catecholamines
## Cyproheptadine (5HT2A, histamine)
	- Pros: cleaner than antipsychotics
	- Cons: fatigue next day at above 1mg
	- It feels a lot cleaner than the antipsychotics like quetiapine and olazapine
	- Fatigue the next day can happen
	- There's a fine threshold where it works well yet not causing the fatigue, around 1 milligram
## Melatonin (adrenaline, melatonin)
	- Pros: cheap, safe to mix
	- Cons: not effective for severe insomnia
	- Notoriously known for being not effective
	- What you wanna try is, right before you go to bed you pop some melatonin and see if that works, making sure you lay down right after
## Lemborexant, Suvorexant (Orexin)
	- Pros: potent, manageable side effects
	- Cons: expensive
	- These are orexin antagonists
	- Orexin is like your awakefullness neurotransmitter; expensive but the sides effects are quite manageable
	- In the beginning it might make you depressed and you might get sleep paralysis 
## Benzodiazepines (GABA-A)
	- Pros: potent
	- Cons: high risk of dependency
## Gabapentin, Pregabalin (GABA)
	- Pros: helps with neuropathic pain
	- Poor cognition the day after
	- Clonazepam, xanax, and advan: these have quite a high risk for abuse and dependency and the withdrawal periods are very long and very harsh
## Zolpidem, Zopiclone (GABA)
	- Pros: potent
	- Cons: abuse potential, memory loss, anxiety and fatigue the day after
	- Z-drugs, help you fall asleep and it can give you some euphoria if you choose to stay up all night; very high abuse potential, not as bad as benzos 
	- It can wipe your memory, feeling anxious and tired the next day
## Phenibut (GABA-B)
	- Pros: nootropic, anxiolytic, and prosocial effect
	- Cons: addictive
## Trazodone (5HT2A, adrenaline, histamine)
	- Pros: broad mechanism of action, relatively low side effects, inexpensive
	- Cons: mild anxiety and brain fog the day after, but only for a short period
	- Braod mechanism of action even at the sleep aid recommended dosage like 25 - 100 milligrams, which is lower side, but it can still work to reduce excess serotonin, norepinephrine, and histamine
	- It can make you a little bit sluggish in the morning, maybe anxious, but it doesn't last all day like quetiapine and olanzipine 
	- Overall a pretty good sleep aid
## Buprenorphine (endorphins)
	- Pros: can be used as needed, minimal risk of dependency with microdose
	- Cons: overstimulating if taken later in the day, easy to overdose and get nauseous
	- You're probably only going to need it if you have been abusing opioids or taking painkillers for extended period of time 
	- If you take it at night, it could be overstimulating
	- It has a pretty long halflife about 36 hours
## Low Dose Naltrexone (endorphins)
	- Pros: may improve other areas of health
	- Cons: has to be taken consistently
	- Can be taken at night, but continuously has to be taken
## Phosphatidyleserine (HPA)
	- Pros: easy to obtain, reliable, safe to mix
	- Cons: generally none
	- Reliable form of sleep aid that's gonna work for adrenal fatigue
	- Supplement
## Diphenhydramine (antihistamine)
	- Pros: easy to obtain, relatively safe to mix
	- Cons: possibly minor fatigue and brain fog the day after
	- Anti histamine
	- Work for minor insomnia
## Cannabis
	- Pros: often works when nothing else does
	- Cons: habit forming, fatigue the day after
	- (marijuana) can be addictive
