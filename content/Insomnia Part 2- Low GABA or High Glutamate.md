## Factors involved in low GABA(gamma-aminobutyric acid) / high glutamate
	- Withdrawal from alcohol, benzos, barbiturates
	- Wireless emissions (EMF)
	- Chronic stress, leaky gut & blood brain barrier
	- Gluten, dairy, MSG, Aspartame
## What may help ( pick up to 3)
	- Magnesium Threonate
	- Vitamin B6 10 mg, during the day
	- Zinc 5 - 12.5 mg, low dose during the day
	- Lemon Balm

## Additional info
	- When you have low GABA, glutamate inversely goes higher, which can make it difficult to fall and stay asleep
	- Other symptoms would be such as: feeling anxious for no reason, not being able to sit still racing thoughts, feeling like brain is on fire, tire but wired, reduced emotional intelligence-- Like reading social cues, feeling shaky, sensory overload-- With bright lights and loud sounds, maybe history of asthma or seizure 
	- Modern lifestyle take a toll on GABA and glutamate gets too high as a result which is neurotoxic
	- Phenibut, benzos, zopiclone, zolpidem, gabapentinoids: when used daily, they can create more problems for later
	- Go off processed foods; gluten, dairy, sugar
	- Zinc has to be low dose, as too much will end up raising glutamate, you cna add this if there's low motivation, depression, or frequently runny nose
	- If there is dry eyes, restlessness, poor dream recall, or tingling extremities, then B6 may be added, should be taken low dose during the day
	- Magnesium if there jaw tension, chocolate cravings, muscle twitches, leg cramps at night, this can be taken before bed
	- NAC is probably better taken earlier in the day, it can make sleep worse for some people
	- Glutamate is an excitatory nerurotransmitter that acts on NMDA, Kainate, and AMPA receptors; it has an inverse relationship with GABA- In ideal balance, glutamate helps with learning and memory recall, but when it gets too high it's excitotocix
