## Lifestyle Changes
	- Avoid high PUFA oils, processed foods, and alcohol
	- Don't overdo caffeine
	- Take care of gut health, fix insulin issue
	- Get adequate sleep, don't over train, reduce stress where possible
## What may lower cortisol
	- Zinc 5 - 15 mg
	- Phosphatidylserine 100 - 500 mg
	- Magnesium 100 - 400 mg
	- Relora 200 - 500 mg
	- Ashwagandha (use sparingly)
	- L-Theanine 100 - 200 mg
	- Aspirin
## Chronically High cortisol
	- Fat midsection, but skinny arms & legs
	- Weight gain in the face
	- Difficulty putting on muscle
	- Insulin resistance, hyperglycemia, constant hunger
	- High blood pressure
	- Frequent colds & infections
	- Suddenly waking up between 2 - 4 AM
	- Acne, oily skin
## Labs & Tests
	- Late night salivary cortisol test
	- UCCR (urinary cortisol to creatinine ratio)
	- UFC (24 hour urinary free cortisol)
	- DST & LNSC (for Cushing's)
## Additional info
	- Despite the cortisol trend, it's good to know what high cortisol actually looks like
	- If you had chronically elevated cortisol or frequent spikes then take a look at chronically high cortisol section
	- You want to do some labs make sure you do in fact have high cortisol at the times of the day when it should be low, and to make sure you don't have Cushing's because then it'd be no longer a simple matter of taking cortisol lowering supplements
	- And we don't want to lower cortisol if it's already low 
	- Low cortisol could look like low blood pressure, POTS, lack of energy and motivation, salt cravings and maybe frequent urination 
	- When we think of PTSD, we'd logically think it's high cortisol but it's usually the opposite because norepinephrine can get out of control without cortisol to buffer it 
	- The cortisol trend is great since it gets people familiar with biochemistry 
	- Just note that it's one of many complexities and it might not be what you are looking for 
	- So we do need to look at the HPA axis later on
