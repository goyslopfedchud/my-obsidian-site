## Supplements
	1. NAC (blend with molybdenum)
	2. Curcumin (theracumin or longvida)
	3. Butyrate: to reduce craving and the rewarding effects of stimulants, may improve impulse control and dopamine receptor recovery
	4. Parthenolide (feverfew): may block the euphoric effects of cocaine
	5. Modafinil: limited research but strong anecdotes in reducing cravings and improving withdrawal symptoms
	- Combining NAC, curcumin, butyrate will apply to marijuana and other stimulants as well, like amphetamines and alcohol
	- The goal here is to reduce intensity of the craving 
	- The scientific evidence around this is low in quantity, but in real life practice they do work
	- Some people don't tolerate NAC- That's probably because they have too much heavy metals in their body
	- Top three if you cycle them it shouldn't really cost much- In the long run by reducing the cravings it'll save you money rather than spending money on the substances
	- Pathenolide is an active compound in the herb called feverfew, so supplements as feverfew
	- Feverfew surprisingly does good job at blocking the euphoric effects of cocaine, to be taken when you have cravings, whereas top three you can cycle them for every third day
	- Feverfew will directly block the serotonin receptor that's associated with coke's euphoria; so use it too much, you'll have a rebound craving
	- Modafinil has great anecdotes on coke craving, it helps with the withdrawal as well, mostly psychological and possibly physical 
## Factors that lowers impulse control and increase drug seeking
	1. Alcohol
	2. Sugar
	3. SSRI
	4. Benzodiazepines
	5. Z drugs (zopiclone, ambien)
	6. Antipsychotics as sleep aid
	7. Trazodone as sleep aid
	8. Yohimbine 
	- These have evidence in anecdotes towards increasing drug seeking or making your impulse control worse
	- If you take the supplements above, you may neglect most of what's in this list 
	- Alcohol and z drugs, they might override the anti craving effects of NAC, curcumin, and butyrate
	- Considering all perspective, addiction to alcohol is possibly worse than cocaine, but you can follow the three supplements method for addiction as well
	- Antipsychotics and trazodone if you take them as sleep aid there is certain rebound effect that might make you more impulsive, possibly crave drugs, maybe restless, the solution is to switch to a different sleep aid or, leave it as you need them
	- SSRIs are known to increase impulsivity or drug seeking
